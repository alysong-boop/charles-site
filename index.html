About

Who is Charles?

Charles is the starter — a living culture of wild yeast and bacteria that was born in Lake Charles, Louisiana.

He's traveled a lot for a jar of flour and water: Louisiana to Italy, and now he lives in Ann Arbor, where he raises every loaf on this page — no commercial yeast, no shortcuts.

I spend a lot of time thinking about everything wrong with the bread aisle — dough conditioners, preservatives, ingredient lists that read like a lab report. This is the opposite of that. Flour, water, salt, time. One batch a month, made by hand, from my kitchen in Ann Arbor.

FAQ

Questions people ask

When and where is pickup?

Pickup windows are Saturday afternoon and Sunday on bake weekend — the first weekend after orders close. Exact location and times come with your order confirmation.

Do you deliver?

Yes — campus-area Ann Arbor only, the same weekend as pickup. If you're not sure you're in range, ask before ordering.

When do orders close? What if I miss the window?

Orders close end of day the first Friday of the month. After that, the batch count is locked — I mix exactly what was ordered. Miss it and you're first in line when the next menu drops.

How do I pay?

Venmo or cash at pickup. No cards, no apps beyond that.

How big is a loaf? Does it come sliced?

Around two pounds, and no — an uncut loaf stays fresh days longer.

How should I store it?

Cut side down on the counter for two to three days, or slice and freeze — it toasts back like new. Never the fridge; refrigeration stales bread faster.

Is sourdough vegan?

The staple loaves are just flour, water, and salt, so yes. Monthly add-ins vary — anything with cheese or honey isn't — so check the ingredient list on each menu item.

What about allergens?

Everything contains wheat. Seeded loaves contain chia, flax, pumpkin seed, hemp, and oats. This is a home kitchen, not an allergen-controlled facility — products may contact nuts, dairy, and eggs. Full ingredients are on every label. If you have a serious allergy, this probably isn't the bread for you, and I'd rather tell you that than find out the hard way.

Is sourdough really easier to digest?

For a lot of people, yes. The long fermentation lets wild yeast and bacteria break down much of the flour's fructans and phytic acid — the compounds that bother many sensitive stomachs — before you ever eat it. It is not gluten-free, though, and it is not safe for celiac disease. Easier is not the same as allergen-free.

Is this a licensed bakery?

No, and legally it doesn't need to be. Charles operates under the Michigan Cottage Food Law, which allows small home-based producers to sell non-hazardous foods like bread directly to their customers — no commercial kitchen, no inspections, in exchange for full transparency: every product is labeled with its complete ingredient list and a statement that it was made in a home kitchen not inspected by the state. It's the same framework behind most farmers market bakers. You're not buying from a factory, and that's the point.
